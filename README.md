102 flashcards so far, grouped by session. New decks are added as the course progresses.
 
## How to study
 
1. Pick a deck, or study all cards. Click a deck name to study only that deck.
2. Read the question and answer it in your head.
3. Tap the card (or press **Space**) to flip it.
4. Be honest: click **I knew it** or **Review again**.
5. Later, turn on **Only "review again"** to practise the cards you missed.
Keyboard shortcuts: **Space** flip · **← →** move · **1** review again · **2** I knew it
 
Your progress is saved in your own browser only. Use the same browser and device each time, or your marks won't be there.
 
## About the exam
 
The **scikit-learn Associate Practitioner** certification is the entry level of Probabl's official scikit-learn certification programme (Associate, Professional, Expert). Probabl is the company behind scikit-learn.
 
- 30 to 35 multiple-choice questions
- 120 minutes
- Passing score: above 70%
- Two parts: theory questions (1–2 points each) and practical application questions based on business cases (2–3 points each)
- Taken online with a remote proctor, or at a test centre, through Kryterion's Webassessor
- During the exam you may use the scikit-learn documentation and a JupyterLite notebook
- Passing earns a digital badge on Credly
Exam details can change. Check the official [study guide](https://certification.probabl.ai/scikit-learn-associate) before you book.
 
### What the exam covers
 
| Section | Topics | In our course |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Machine learning concepts | Supervised, unsupervised and semi-supervised learning; model families (linear, tree-based, ensemble, neighbours); features, labels, train and test sets; overfitting and underfitting; bias/variance trade-off | Started in Session 1 |
| 2. Data preprocessing | Loading parquet files; basic plots; spotting wrongly encoded columns; `SimpleImputer`; feature scaling (`StandardScaler`, `MinMaxScaler`); `OrdinalEncoder` and `OneHotEncoder`; `ColumnTransformer` | Scaling and `ColumnTransformer` in Session 3 |
| 3. Model building and evaluation | `train_test_split`, `fit()`, `predict()`; accuracy, precision, recall, F1, confusion matrix, mean squared error, R²; comparing with dummy models | `fit()` and `predict()` in Sessions 2–3 |
| 4. Model selection and validation | Cross-validation (`KFold`, `ShuffleSplit`); learning and validation curves; `GridSearchCV` and `RandomizedSearchCV`; stability of coefficients | Coming with supervised learning |
| 5. Interpretation and communication | Visualizing results with matplotlib and seaborn; explaining models and metrics to non-technical stakeholders | Radar charts in Session 3 |
 
**Tip:** unsupervised learning is on the syllabus, so don't skip the clustering decks.
